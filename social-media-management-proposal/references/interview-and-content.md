# Interview & Content Generation

## Questions to cover (skip any the user already answered)

**Client & context**
- Client/brand name and industry
- Current situation: why are they looking at social media management now? (no presence, inconsistent posting, wrong platforms for their audience, need to scale an existing effort, rebrand, etc.)

**Scope of service** — confirm explicitly, don't assume:
- Organic content creation & posting?
- Community management (responding to comments/DMs, engagement)?
- Paid social ads?
- Influencer/creator partnerships?
- Analytics & reporting?
- Any combination of the above — get the exact list, since it drives which sections even apply.

**Platforms**
- Does the client already know which platforms they want, or should Claude recommend based on objective/audience/industry?
- Any platforms explicitly excluded (e.g., "no TikTok")?

**Objectives**
- Primary goal: awareness, community growth, engagement, lead gen, driving sales, etc.
- Any secondary goals or constraints (e.g., "must grow follower count by X", "launch before [date]")

**Budget & pricing model**
- Retainer (monthly fee), tiered packages (e.g., Starter/Growth/Premium), or project-based?
- The actual number(s) to work with, and whether paid ad spend is separate from the management fee (it usually is — keep these distinct in the pricing section)

**Timeline**
- Contract length / initial term, launch date if known

**Branding**
- Brand colors (hex if available), logo file if they have one to upload, or an existing deck to match the style of
- Tone: formal enterprise pitch vs. a scrappier small-business/creator-brand proposal — affects copy tone, not just visuals

**Section structure**
- Confirm which sections from the default flow to use, and whether to add anything client-specific (e.g., a competitor social audit, a case study, an influencer roster) — don't assume the full default list applies to every client.

## Generating each content section

**Platform Strategy** — recommend platforms based on where the target audience actually spends time and what the objective needs (e.g., LinkedIn for B2B lead gen, TikTok/Instagram Reels for awareness with a younger audience, Pinterest for a product-discovery-driven ecommerce brand). Explain the "why" briefly per platform — avoid listing platforms without reasoning.

**Content Strategy / Pillars** — propose 3-5 content pillars (recurring themes/categories) tied to the objective and brand, not generic filler ("educational", "behind-the-scenes", "user-generated content", etc. — but made specific to this client's actual business).

**Posting Cadence & Sample Calendar** — propose a realistic cadence per platform (varies a lot — e.g., daily for TikTok/Instagram, a few times a week for LinkedIn) and show a sample week or two visually (a calendar grid works well in the deck), not just a bullet list of frequencies.

**Deliverables & Scope** — be explicit and itemized: number of posts/month per platform, whether that includes stories/reels, whether copywriting and design are included, whether community management response-time commitments are part of scope, what's explicitly *not* included. This section prevents disputes later — precision matters more than polish here.

**Pricing Packages / Tiers** — if tiered, lay out what differs between tiers (platforms covered, post volume, paid ad management, reporting frequency) so the client can see the value step-up, not just a price difference. If it's a single retainer, present it clearly with what's included.

**Expected Results** — generate reasonable estimated ranges (not single precise numbers) tied to the objective (e.g., follower growth range, engagement rate range, or for paid-inclusive scopes, reach/CPA ranges as in `meta-ads-proposal`). Always:
- Present as a range, not false precision.
- Label clearly as an estimate, not a guarantee, both in the deck copy and in chat.
- If the user supplies historical data or benchmarks, use those instead of generic estimates.

**Next Steps / CTA** — summarize the ask (which package/pricing, contract term, what happens if they approve) and a clear, decisive call to action.
