# Interview & Content Generation

## Questions to cover (skip any the user already answered)

**Client & context**
- Client/brand name and industry
- Current situation: why are they looking at Meta ads now? (new to paid social, scaling up, underperforming current efforts, launching a new product, etc.)

**Objectives**
- Primary goal: awareness, traffic, leads, sales/conversions, app installs, etc.
- Any secondary goals or constraints (e.g., "must stay under $X CPA", "launch before [date]")

**Budget & timeline**
- Total budget or monthly budget
- Campaign duration / flight dates if known

**Audience**
- Does the client have a defined target audience, or should Claude propose one?
- Any exclusions or must-includes (existing customers, specific geos, age ranges, etc.)

**Branding**
- Brand colors (hex if available), logo file if they have one to upload, or an existing deck to match the style of
- Tone: is this a formal enterprise pitch or a scrappier small-business proposal? Affects copy tone, not just visuals.

**Optional**
- Include a "Why Us" / credibility section? Default is no — only include if requested or content is supplied (case studies, past results, team info). Don't invent agency credentials.

## Generating each content section

**Proposed Strategy** — translate the objective into a Meta-specific approach: which campaign objectives/placements make sense (e.g., Advantage+ shopping campaigns for ecommerce sales, lead gen forms for leads, reach/brand awareness campaigns for awareness), and why, in a sentence or two the client will understand — avoid jargon-only bullet lists.

**Target Audience** — if the user gave specifics, use them. If proposing one, base it on the objective/industry and clearly frame it as a recommendation ("Recommended audience — to validate with client") rather than presenting it as already agreed.

**Budget & Channel Breakdown** — split the budget across the proposed campaign types/placements (e.g., prospecting vs. retargeting, Facebook vs. Instagram) in a way that matches the strategy. Show this as a chart, not just a table.

**Timeline / Phasing** — if the campaign has phases (e.g., launch/testing → optimization → scale), show them on a simple timeline. If it's a single flat flight, a simple start/end is fine — don't invent phases that aren't needed.

**Expected Results / Projections** — generate reasonable estimated ranges (not single precise numbers) for the objective's key metric(s) based on the stated budget and general industry patterns for that objective type (e.g., typical CPM/CPC ranges by objective). Always:
- Present as a range, not a false-precision single number.
- Label clearly as an estimate ("Projected range, based on [budget] and industry benchmarks — not a guarantee").
- If the user supplies their own historical data or benchmarks, use those instead of generic industry patterns — real data beats generic estimates.

**Next Steps / Investment & CTA** — summarize the ask (budget, timeline, what happens if they approve) and a clear call to action. Keep this slide simple and decisive — it's the close.
