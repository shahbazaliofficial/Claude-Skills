# Action Plan Framework

## Prioritization

Score each finding on two axes:

- **Expected impact**: how much this is likely to move the account's key metric (based on the size/severity of the issue found — a tracking gap undercounting a large share of conversions is high-impact; a slightly suboptimal ad set naming convention is not).
- **Effort/risk**: how much work or risk is involved (a budget reallocation between existing ad sets is low-effort/low-risk; rebuilding account structure from scratch is high-effort and carries delivery-disruption risk).

## Sequencing

Even high-impact items need the right order — recommend fixes in this sequence, not just by impact score alone:

1. **Tracking/measurement fixes** — always first; nothing else can be trusted or properly evaluated until this is solid.
2. **Structural fixes** — audience overlap, CBO/ABO mismatches, funnel-stage separation — fix the foundation before optimizing what runs on top of it.
3. **Delivery/learning-phase fixes** — stop actions that are actively preventing the algorithm from optimizing (excessive edits, budget too fragmented to exit learning).
4. **Creative and testing** — once the foundation is sound, this is usually the highest-leverage lever for actually improving results.
5. **Budget scaling** — scale what's proven to work, using the pacing guidance in `budget-bidding-scaling.md`; scaling before the foundation is fixed just amplifies existing inefficiency.

## Format

Present the plan as a short, ordered list of concrete actions, each with:
- What to do (specific — which campaign/ad set/creative, not "optimize your ads")
- Why (which finding it addresses)
- Expected impact, framed directionally and with appropriate uncertainty, not as a guarantee
- What to monitor to confirm it's working

Group by the sequencing above (or explicitly note when something can happen in parallel), and lead with the top 3-5 items rather than dumping every possible improvement at once — a marketer needs to know what to do *first*, not everything that could theoretically be improved.

## For ongoing/recurring reviews

Instead of rebuilding the full plan from scratch each time:
- Note which previous action items were implemented and what happened as a result (if that's known/reported back)
- Surface any new findings since the last review
- Update the plan: close out completed items, keep still-relevant ones, add new ones — rather than presenting an entirely fresh list disconnected from what came before
