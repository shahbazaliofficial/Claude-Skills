# Budget, Bidding & Scaling

## Budget allocation

- Compare spend share to results share by campaign/ad set — a campaign getting 40% of budget but producing 15% of results is a reallocation candidate; one producing disproportionately more results than its budget share may be under-funded relative to its potential.
- Check this by funnel stage too, not just by campaign name — prospecting and retargeting budgets should reflect the account's actual funnel needs (a brand-new account has little retargeting pool yet and should be prospecting-heavy; a mature account with strong traffic may be under-investing in retargeting).

## Bid strategy fit

- Lowest cost / highest volume (no cap) — best when the goal is maximum volume within budget and there's no hard efficiency ceiling; can drift toward less efficient results as budget scales if left unchecked.
- Cost cap / bid cap — useful once there's enough data to know a defensible target cost, to prevent efficiency from drifting as spend increases; too tight a cap can choke delivery if set below what the auction actually requires.
- ROAS goal (for value-optimized campaigns) — useful for ecommerce/revenue-focused accounts once there's enough purchase volume for Meta to optimize against reliably; unreliable on low purchase volume.

Match the recommendation to the account's actual data volume — recommending a cost cap or ROAS goal on an account with too little conversion volume to support it will likely hurt delivery, not help it.

## Scaling budget without resetting performance

- Large, sudden budget increases (commonly cited: more than ~20% at once) can trigger a new learning phase or destabilize delivery — recommend incremental increases (e.g., 15-20% every few days) over large jumps when scaling a working ad set/campaign.
- Horizontal scaling (adding new ad sets/campaigns targeting adjacent audiences) is often more stable than pure vertical scaling (just raising budget on the same ad set) once an ad set's efficiency starts to degrade at higher spend — recommend this as an alternative when vertical scaling has plateaued or reversed.
- Distinguish "this ad set is ready to scale" (consistently meeting the target metric over a real time window, not just one good day) from "this ad set had one good day" — scaling on noisy short-term data is a common, avoidable mistake worth flagging if you see it being considered.

## Sequencing a scale-up recommendation

When recommending scaling as an action item, be specific: which campaign/ad set, by roughly how much, over what pacing, and what to watch (CPA/ROAS trend, frequency, learning phase status) to confirm it's holding before scaling further. A vague "increase budget" recommendation isn't actionable — give the plan, not just the direction.
