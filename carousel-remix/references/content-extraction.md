# Content Extraction

## Why structure-first matters

Extracting "what each slide is doing" rather than "what each slide says" is what keeps the rewrite original. If you work from the source's exact sentences, even careful edits tend to stay close to the original phrasing. Working from the *role* of each slide forces you to actually re-derive the content in your own words.

## Typical carousel slide roles

Most creator carousels follow recognizable patterns — identify which role each slide is playing:

- **Cover/hook** — the first slide, designed to stop the scroll and promise a payoff (a bold claim, a question, a surprising stat, a "save this" framing).
- **Context/problem** — sets up why this topic matters or what problem it addresses.
- **Point slides** — the core content, often one idea per slide (a tip, a step, a reason, a mistake to avoid).
- **Proof/stat slide** — a statistic, example, or mini case study supporting the argument.
- **List/framework slide** — a numbered or bulleted breakdown, sometimes a named framework.
- **CTA/closing** — a wrap-up, summary, or call to action (follow, comment, save, link in bio, etc.)

Not every carousel has all of these, and some slides blend roles — that's fine, just note what each slide is actually doing.

## What to capture per slide

For each slide, note:
1. Its role (from the list above, or describe it if it doesn't fit neatly)
2. The core idea/claim it's making, paraphrased at a high level (a phrase or two, not a copied sentence)
3. Anything structurally distinctive worth carrying forward (e.g., "uses a numbered list of 5 items", "poses a question then answers it")

Do NOT capture or carry forward the source's exact sentences, taglines, or distinctive phrasing — that's the input to step 2 (extraction), not something to paste into the rewrite.

## Handling ambiguous or partial source material

- Screenshots with cut-off or small text: read what's legible, state plainly what wasn't readable, and ask if it matters (sometimes a partially-visible slide is enough to infer its role even if exact wording is unclear).
- A link that won't fetch (login wall): don't guess at the content — ask the user to paste text or screenshots instead.
- A source with more visual/design content than text (e.g., mostly an illustration with a short caption): note the caption's message and treat the slide as light on text content — the rewrite doesn't need to pad it with more words than the original had.
